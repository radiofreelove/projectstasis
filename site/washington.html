Policy update

Update, Nov. 17, 2026: Washington delays work-rule enforcement

Washington's announcement: On Nov. 17, 2026, HCA announced Washington will pursue delayed enforcement of the work rules. Through June 30, 2027, people in Apple Health for Adults will not lose or be denied coverage for not meeting the work rules. Six-month renewals and the 1-month past-bills limit still start Jan. 1, 2027. Source: HCA announcement on delayed enforcement, Nov. 17, 2026

Federal choice: The work rules and checking still start Jan. 1, 2027. On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave states this hold harmless choice through June 30, 2027, subject to federal approval. Source: Federal letter to state Medicaid directors, Nov. 13, 2026 (SMD# 26-006)

What it does not do: This choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

Court case: On Nov. 5, 2026, Washington, Oregon, and two other states sued the federal government over the work rules (State of Oregon v. Oz, No. 6:26-cv-02435). Oral argument is set for Jan. 12, 2027. As of Nov. 21, 2026, no court order has changed these rules. Source: WA Attorney General litigation tracker Source: Oregon DOJ tracker, State of Oregon v. Oz Source: U.S. District Court (D. Or.) calendar, Jan. 12, 2027

Deadline

Have medical bills? Read this before Jan. 1, 2027.

Before Jan. 1, 2027, Apple Health may pay bills from up to 3 months before the month you apply. Source: HCA changes coming to Apple Health

On Jan. 1, 2027, that time gets shorter. For Apple Health for Adults, it is 1 month. Source: HCA changes coming to Apple Health

The short version

  • The name: Apple Health is Washington's name for Medicaid.
  • What's new: New federal work rules for Apple Health for Adults start Jan. 1, 2027. Source: HCA changes coming to Apple Health
  • Right now: The Health Care Authority (HCA) says there is nothing you need to do right now.
  • Who: Only people in Apple Health for Adults. That means ages 19 to 64, no Medicare, living in Washington, with low income. Source: WAC 182-505-0250 (Apple Health adults)
  • Records first: Washington checks its own data first. HCA estimated that it could check about 64 percent of people in Apple Health for Adults this way, or find they are exempt.
  • Shorter coverage: Coverage periods go from 12 months to 6 months. That starts at a person's first renewal in 2027.
  • Notices: HCA sends notices by mail or email. Keep your contact information up to date.

Meet the rules any one way

  • Income Your household has at least $580 in income a month. Source: HCA work requirements page
    or
  • School You are in an educational program at least half-time. Source: HCA work rules fact sheet, June 2026
    or
  • 80 hours A work program, community service, or a mix of activities. The total is at least 80 hours a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

Any 1 way meets the rules.

The rules, in plain words

You may not have to if you

  • Are pregnant, or in the 12 months after a pregnancy Source: HCA work rules fact sheet, June 2026
  • Are a parent, caretaker, or guardian of a child under 14 Source: HCA work requirements page
  • Are medically frail Source: HCA work rules fact sheet, June 2026
  • Are American Indian or Alaska Native
  • Are a foster youth, or a former foster youth under 26

Many people do not have to meet the rule at all. HCA's fact sheet calls these reasons exemptions. See every reason

What to do

  1. Take the 1-minute check. Your answers stay on your device.
  2. Apply online, by phone, on paper, or in person. Apple Health enrollment is open all year.
  3. You may tell the state your own income, work, or exemption status. The state accepts what you tell it.

Bottom line

Only Washington decides

This guide says "you may." Only Washington decides who meets the rules or is exempt.
