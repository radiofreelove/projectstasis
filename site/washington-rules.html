The new Apple Health rules, in plain words

Review status

Some sources on this page were read with a research tool. A person has not yet read each one from start to finish. If this guide and the state say different things, follow the state, and tell us. See every source.

This page explains who the rules are for and the 6 ways to meet them.

Not sure if the rules fit you? Take the 1-minute check.

The short version

  • Starts: Jan. 1, 2027. The Health Care Authority (HCA) says there is nothing you need to do right now. Source: HCA changes coming to Apple Health
  • Who: Only people in Apple Health for Adults. Source: WAC 182-505-0250 (Apple Health adults)
  • When the state looks: The month before you apply. At a renewal, one of the 6 months before. Source: HCA work rules fact sheet, June 2026
  • Records first: Washington checks its own data first.
  • Names: HCA calls these rules work requirements. The federal name is community engagement.

A new choice for states

On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave each state a choice. Through June 30, 2027, a state may choose not to turn anyone down, and not to end anyone's coverage, only for not meeting the work rules. The state must still check whether each person meets the rules or does not have to. The federal name for this is a hold harmless period. Source: Federal letter to state Medicaid directors, Nov. 13, 2026 (SMD# 26-006)

The rules still start Jan. 1, 2027. The choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

A state has to ask the federal Medicaid agency and get its approval. The choice covers applications the state gets, and renewals the state starts, through June 30, 2027. A state may pick a shorter time. A state that wants it from Jan. 1, 2027 was asked to tell the agency by Dec. 11, 2026. Source: Federal letter to state Medicaid directors, Nov. 13, 2026 (SMD# 26-006)

As of Nov. 14, 2026, HCA's pages about the new rules did not say what Washington will choose.

Who the rules are for

The work rules apply only to Apple Health for Adults. That group is people who:

  • Are age 19 to 64
  • Do not have Medicare
  • Live in Washington
  • Have income under 138 percent of the federal poverty level

Meet the rules any one way

HCA's work requirements page lists 6 ways. You need only one.

Good to know

1. Income

Your household has at least $580 in income a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

Where $580 comes from: 80 hours times the federal minimum wage of $7.25.

Work only part of the year? Seasonal workers may use an average of $580 a month over 6 months.

This is your household's monthly MAGI income (Modified Adjusted Gross Income), the same income HCA counts for Apple Health. It counts everyone in your household together. It does not have to come from a job.

Good to know

2. Work

You work at least 80 hours a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

Paid work, self-employment, in-kind work, and unpaid work can count. In-kind work means work paid in goods, housing, or services, such as lower rent.

Not sure how your job counts? Ask HCA or a free helper.

Good to know

3. School

You are in an educational program at least half-time. Source: HCA work rules fact sheet, June 2026

Under the federal rule, this includes high school and GED programs.

Taking fewer credits? You can mix school credits with other hours in way 6 below.

Good to know

4. A work program

You are in a work program at least 80 hours a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

Good to know

5. Community service

You do community service at least 80 hours a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

Good to know

6. A mix

You do a mix of activities. The total is at least 80 hours a month. Source: HCA work requirements page

You may add up hours of work, community service, a work program, and school.

If you take 1 to 6 school credits, HCA converts your credits into monthly hours so you can add them to other activities: Source: HCA work requirements page

How the state checks

  • Records first: Washington checks its own data first. HCA estimated that it could check about 64 percent of people in Apple Health for Adults this way, or find they are exempt.
  • Your word: You may state your own income, work, or exemption status. The state accepts this. HCA calls it self-attestation.
  • Shorter coverage: Coverage periods go from 12 months to 6 months. That starts at a person's first renewal in 2027.
