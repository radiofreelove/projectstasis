Policy update

Update, Nov. 13, 2026: A new choice for states

Still starting: The work or activity rules still start Jan. 1, 2027. The checking starts then too.

What's new: The federal government gave each state a choice. Through June 30, 2027, a state may choose not to turn anyone down, and not to end anyone's coverage, only for not meeting the work or activity rules. The state must still check whether each person meets the rules or does not have to. Source: Federal letter to state Medicaid directors, Nov. 13, 2026 (SMD# 26-006)

What it does not do: This choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

Utah: As of Nov. 21, 2026, Utah's pages about the new rules did not say what Utah will choose. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page Source: Utah Medicaid H.R. 1 page

Deadline

Have medical bills? Read this before Jan. 1, 2027.

Through Dec. 31, 2026, most Utah Medicaid programs can cover care from up to 3 months before the month you apply. Source: Utah Medicaid H.R. 1 page

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, Adult Expansion Medicaid can cover care from 1 month before the month you apply. Source: Utah Medicaid H.R. 1 page

The short version

  • What's new: Starting Jan. 1, 2027, some adults ages 19 to 64 must meet new work or activity rules to get Utah Medicaid. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page
  • Which programs: Adult Expansion Medicaid and Targeted Adult Medicaid (TAM).
  • Utah's name for the rules: the community engagement requirement.
  • When the state looks: The month before you apply. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page
  • Records first: The state checks records it already has before it asks you for anything.

Meet the rules any one way

  • Income Utah's pages say you earn at least $580 a month. The federal rule counts combined household income. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page Source: Federal rule on the work requirement (91 Fed. Reg. 33348)
    or
  • School You go to school at least half-time. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page
    or
  • 80 hours You spend 80 hours a month on work, job training, volunteering, or school. Add them up. Source: Utah Medicaid community engagement page

Any 1 way meets the rules.

The rules, in plain words

You may not have to if you

  • Are pregnant, or get Medicaid for the months after a birth Source: Utah exclusions and exceptions table
  • Care for a child 13 or younger, or for a person with a disability Source: Utah exclusions and exceptions table
  • Have a disability, or a health condition that makes daily tasks hard Source: Utah exclusions and exceptions table
  • Are American Indian or Alaska Native
  • Are in foster care, or were

There are more reasons. Utah calls them exclusions and exceptions. See every reason

What to do

  1. Take the 1-minute check. Your answers stay on your device.
  2. Apply online, on paper, or in person.
  3. If the state cannot confirm your activity or your reason from its records, you can report it yourself.

Bottom line

Apply if you think you qualify

If you think you qualify, apply. If the state says no, you can apply again.
