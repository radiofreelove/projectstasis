Immigration status and Utah Medicaid

A federal law changed who can get Utah Medicaid on Oct. 1, 2026. This page explains what Utah has said.

The short version

  • What changed: Since Oct. 1, 2026, Utah Medicaid is mostly for 4 groups of people. They are listed below.
  • Children and pregnancy: Utah says children and people who are pregnant may still qualify for health insurance.
  • Emergencies: Emergency Medicaid can cover a sudden, life-threatening emergency.
  • This site: It never asks about your immigration status.

Who Utah Medicaid is mostly for now

  • U.S. citizens and U.S. nationals
  • People with a green card who have been in the U.S. for 5 years or more
  • People from Cuba or Haiti who entered under special rules
  • Citizens of the Marshall Islands, Micronesia, or Palau (Utah calls them COFA migrants)

Who may lose Utah Medicaid

Utah says the change affects groups that could get Medicaid before. These include refugees, people granted asylum, people with humanitarian parole, and survivors of trafficking or domestic violence.

Utah said it would send each affected member a letter by Oct. 1, 2026. The letter explains the change and how to appeal.

You have 90 days from the date on a notice to ask for a hearing. How to ask for a hearing

Emergency Medicaid

  • It is for people who meet the income rules for Medicaid but not the citizenship rules.
  • It covers only a sudden, life-threatening emergency.
  • It covers giving birth. It does not cover care after the birth.
