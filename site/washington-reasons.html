Reasons you may not have to meet the Apple Health rules

The Health Care Authority (HCA) calls these exemptions. This page lists them.

The short version

  • Many people: Many people do not have to meet the rule at all.
  • Records first: Washington checks its own data first.
  • Your word: You may state your own exemption status. The state accepts this.
  • Who decides: This guide says "you may." Only Washington decides who is exempt.

Reasons HCA lists

  • You are pregnant, or you are in the 12 months after a pregnancy.
  • You are under age 19.
  • You are a foster youth, or a former foster youth under age 26.
  • You are American Indian or Alaska Native. HCA's list also names certain Tribal populations.
  • You are a parent, caretaker, or guardian of a child under age 14.
  • You are a parent, caretaker, or guardian of a person with a disability.
  • You are a household member of someone who gets SNAP food assistance or TANF cash assistance.
  • You are entitled to Medicare Part A or Part B.
  • You are in jail or prison, or you got out in the last 90 days.
  • You are medically frail. More about health conditions
  • You are in treatment for alcohol or substance use.
  • You are a disabled veteran with a 100 percent total disability rating.
  • You are in a short-term hardship.

Short-term hardships

HCA lists short-term hardship as an exemption. Its papers for clients do not say which kinds count in Washington.

Federal law lets states excuse people for these kinds:

  • Certain care in a hospital or nursing facility
  • Travel for care that you cannot get at home
  • Living in a county where the unemployment rate is 8 percent or more, or 1.5 times the national average
  • Living in a county with a federally declared emergency or disaster

Think one of these fits you? Ask HCA what counts.

American Indian and Alaska Native people

  • Work rules: HCA lists American Indian and Alaska Native people as exempt.
  • Coverage periods: They are also left out of the shorter 6-month coverage periods.

How to tell the state

  1. Washington checks its own data first.
  2. You may state your own exemption status. The state accepts this. HCA calls it self-attestation.
  3. Have a question about your case? Call HCA at 1-800-562-3022.

In the news

Washington is one of the states that sued over the federal rule. The federal rule had an extra test. It tied medical frailty to a person's ability to comply with the work rules. A court order removed that test for the states that sued.
