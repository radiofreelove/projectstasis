The new Utah Medicaid rules, in plain words

This page explains who the rules are for and the 3 ways to meet them.

The short version

  • Starts: Jan. 1, 2027.
  • Who: Adults ages 19 to 64 in Adult Expansion Medicaid or Targeted Adult Medicaid (TAM).
  • If you apply: On or after Jan. 1, 2027, you must meet the rules to get coverage.
  • If you already have it: The rules start at your review, for renewals due in March 2027 or later.
  • When the state looks: The month before you apply.
  • More reviews: Starting Jan. 1, 2027, adults in these 2 programs get a review every 6 months, not every 12.

A new choice for states

On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave each state a choice. Through June 30, 2027, a state may choose not to turn anyone down, and not to end anyone's coverage, only for not meeting the work or activity rules. The state must still check whether each person meets the rules or does not have to. The federal name for this is a hold harmless period.

The rules still start Jan. 1, 2027. The choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

A state has to ask the federal Medicaid agency and get its approval. The choice covers applications the state gets, and renewals the state starts, through June 30, 2027. A state may pick a shorter time. A state that wants it from Jan. 1, 2027 was asked to tell the agency by Dec. 11, 2026.

As of Nov. 14, 2026, Utah's pages about the new rules did not say what Utah will choose.

Meet the rules any one way

1. Income

You earn at least $580 a month.

That is about $135 a week.

Utah's pages do not say whose income counts. The federal rule counts the income of everyone in your household, added together. Ask the state how it counts yours.

Work only part of the year? Utah can use your average income over 6 months.

Where $580 comes from: the federal minimum wage ($7.25 an hour) times 80 hours.

2. School

You go to school at least half-time.

Your school decides what counts as half-time.

3. 80 hours a month

You spend 80 hours a month on work, job training, volunteering, or school. You can add them together.

About 20 hours a week gets you there.

What counts as work or volunteering

Utah's pages do not yet say exactly what counts. The federal rule says volunteering means unpaid work with an organized program that helps the community.

Not sure if what you do counts? Ask the state or a free helper.

Good to know

  • Records first: The state checks records it already has, like work, school, and other benefits, before it asks you for anything.
  • Proof at reviews: You may need to show proof of your activities every 6 months, at your review.
  • If the state asks: You may need to send a form within 30 days, or you could lose your coverage.
