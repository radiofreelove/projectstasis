Reasons you may not have to meet the Utah rules

Utah calls these exclusions and exceptions. This page lists them and how the state checks them.

The short version

  • 2 words: Utah calls these reasons exclusions and exceptions.
  • When the state checks: In the month you apply or renew.
  • You must ask: For 2 hardships, you have to ask for the exception.
  • Tribal members: Utah's letter says to send proof of your status by Jan. 1, 2027.

The rules do not apply to

  • People 18 or younger, or 65 or older.
  • People who have Medicare Part A or Part B.
  • People in some other Medicaid groups, like parents with low income or people who get SSI.
  • People in Medicaid programs other than Adult Expansion Medicaid and TAM.

Reasons Utah lists

Utah calls these exclusions.

  • You are pregnant, or you get Medicaid for the months after a birth (postpartum Medicaid).
  • You are a parent, guardian, or caregiver for a child 13 or younger, or for a person with a disability.
  • You are blind or have a disability. Or you have a mental health condition, a substance use disorder, or a condition that makes daily tasks hard. Utah calls this medically frail or special needs. More about health conditions
  • You are in a drug or alcohol treatment and rehabilitation program.
  • You are American Indian or Alaska Native. See what proof to send.
  • You are in foster care, or you were. Some Utah pages say this means people in the Former Foster Care program. Ask the state which one fits you.
  • You are a veteran with a 100% disability.
  • You have to meet work rules for SNAP or TANF.
  • You are in jail or prison now, or you got out in the last 90 days.

Short-term hardships

Utah says you may qualify for an exception for a month when one of these was true.

  • You were a patient in a hospital, or you lived in a nursing home or another care facility. You must ask for this one.
  • You had to travel outside your community for medical care for you or a dependent. You must ask for this one.
  • Your county had a federal emergency or disaster declaration, or high unemployment.

American Indian and Alaska Native members

  • Send proof: The state must verify your status. Utah's letter says to send proof by Jan. 1, 2027.
  • Reviews: Once your status is verified, your review stays once every 12 months.
  • If it is not verified: You may have to meet the work or activity rules.

Utah's letter lists these kinds of proof. Send one copy for each tribal member in your home.

  • A tribal ID or enrollment card, or the number
  • A Certificate of Indian Blood signed by the Bureau of Indian Affairs
  • An Indian Health Service (IHS) face sheet with your name and date of birth
  • A tribal court paper with your name and date of birth
  • Any of these for a parent or grandparent, plus a birth certificate or court paper that shows how you are related

Ways to send papers to the state

How to tell the state

  1. The state checks records it already has first. If it can confirm your reason, you do not need to do anything.
  2. If it cannot, you can report it online in myCase, on your application or renewal form, or on form 742, the Community Engagement Declaration.
  3. Utah's page names form 742 but does not link to it. Ask the Department of Workforce Services (DWS) for it at 1-866-435-7414.

In the news

On Oct. 29, 2026, a federal court threw out part of a federal rule that narrowed the health reason. Utah was not one of the states in that case. As of Nov. 10, 2026, Utah's pages had not said what changes. Tell the state about your health condition either way.

Why this guide exists

Utah explains the new Medicaid rules across more than a dozen pages and papers. This guide puts what applicants need in one place, in plain words. Each page lists the official sources behind it.
