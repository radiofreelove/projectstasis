Every source we use

This is the full list of sources and links behind the Apple Health guide. Each one shows the date on it and the date we checked it.

  • Read by us: "Yes" means the document was read in full for our research notes. "No" means it was read only in part, or not at all. Each note says which.
  • Not finished: A person has not yet read each Washington source from start to finish.
  • Fact log: To see which fact comes from which source, read the public fact log.
  • Disagreements: Where official papers say different things, see the corrections log.

Federal, court, and site sources

  1. Medicaid Program; Community Engagement Requirement for Certain Individuals (interim final rule), 91 Fed. Reg. 33348
    Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. Date on it: Published June 3, 2026; effective July 31, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-02. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read the first fifth of the document (preamble sections I to II.C.8). Source for the $580 income test (not less than $7.25 times 80, household income), half-time school including high school and GED programs, what counts as work and community service, and the credit-hour conversion.
  2. Order of Vacatur and Enjoinment, Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz, No. 1:26-cv-12962 (D. Mass. Oct. 29, 2026), Doc. 148
    U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts (via CourtListener). Date on it: Oct. 29, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-10. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Vacates the federal rule's medically frail clause tied to ability to comply; enjoins applying it to plaintiff states; bars penalizing plaintiff states for implementation errors or delays for six months. No later docket entries seen in CourtListener on Nov. 2, 2026. Re-read in full on 2026-11-10. The bar on applying the clause, and the 6-month bar on penalties, name the plaintiff states only.
  3. Memorandum and Order on cross-motions for summary judgment, Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz, Doc. 147

  8. SMD# 26-006, Optional Hold Harmless Period for Community Engagement Implementation (State Medicaid Director letter) PDF
    Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. Date on it: Nov. 13, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-14. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-14: 4 pages, 3 footnotes, signed by Daniel Brillman, Deputy Administrator and Director. Offers every state an optional hold harmless period through June 30, 2027. A state that elects it may suspend denials of eligibility and disenrollments when an applicable individual does not demonstrate compliance with community engagement, for applications received and renewals initiated through and including June 30, 2027, or a shorter period the state implements. A state may use the period only if it assesses whether each individual meets the requirement (including deemed compliance) or is excluded, for all relevant applications and renewals in the period. The period covers only conditioning eligibility on community engagement (section 1902(xx)(1) of the Act, 42 C.F.R. 435.559); it does not apply to any other part of implementing community engagement by Jan. 1, 2027, or to denials and disenrollments on other eligibility criteria, such as age or household income. A state requests the period in its mitigation plan and must have CMS approval before using it; a state that intends to use it from Jan. 1, 2027 is asked to notify CMS by Dec. 11, 2026. For applications submitted and renewals initiated after the period ends in a state, the state must deny or terminate eligibility for not meeting the requirement, after the noncompliance procedures at 42 C.F.R. 435.558. The letter does not name any state that has elected the period. Terms: the letter calls the requirement 'community engagement' and says CMS refers to Public Law No. 119-21 as the 'Working Families Tax Cut' (WFTC) legislation. Footnote 3 cites Massachusetts v. Oz as No. 26-12692-RGS; the order in this registry (S28) carries No. 1:26-cv-12962. medicaid.gov answered 403 to a plain automated request on 2026-11-14; the PDF was fetched with a web fetch tool and its text extracted with pdftotext. The CMS press release of the same day is not registered: the letter is the primary source and no page links to the release.

Why this guide exists

The facts about the new Apple Health rules are spread across many official pages and papers. This guide puts what applicants need in one place, in plain words. Each page lists the official sources behind it.

Independent. Not run by Washington's government, the Health Care Authority (HCA), or DSHS. Only Washington decides who meets the rules.

Private. No accounts, ads, cookies, or tracking. How your privacy is protected.
