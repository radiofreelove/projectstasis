Every source we use

This is the full list of sources and links behind the Apple Health guide. Each one shows the date on it and the date we checked it.

  • Read by us: "Yes" means the document was read in full for our research notes. "No" means it was read only in part, or not at all. Each note says which.
  • Not finished: A person has not yet read each Washington source from start to finish.
  • Fact log: To see which fact comes from which source, read the public fact log.
  • Disagreements: Where official papers say different things, see the corrections log.

  12. Changes of circumstance effect on eligibility
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: Revised April 8, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.

  13. Submit my verification
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: No date shown. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13. Key points: after an application or renewal, a letter may ask for more information (a Post Eligibility Review, or PER); HCA staff confirm eligibility based on income, citizenship, or lawful presence. A person has 15 days from the date the letter was mailed, and may ask for more time by email or at 1-800-562-3022. A person must answer even if they just updated their information. Coverage may close if they do not. Documents can be uploaded in the Healthplanfinder document center, emailed, faxed to 1-855-867-4467, or mailed. The read confirms claims WA-APP-09, WA-LTR-02, WA-WRD-05, and WA-LOG-03.
  14. Washington Attorney General's Federal Litigation Tracker
    Office of the Attorney General. Date on it: Says "As of Nov. 5, 2026". Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 (the introduction and all 85 case entries). Item 62 confirms Washington joined Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz on June 29, 2026 with 23 other attorneys general and two governors, and links the filed complaint and the Attorney General's news release on the ruling. Together with shared sources S28 and S40, this supports the statement that Washington is one of the plaintiff states. Also on the page, and not on any guide page: item 85, State of Oregon v. Oz (D. Or., filed Nov. 5, 2026 with three other states), a challenge to the work requirements themselves; and item 83, a motion to intervene filed Oct. 16, 2026 in Neighborhood House v. Department of Health and Human Services, where a Sept. 30, 2026 order requires continued Medicaid funding for certain noncitizens who get SSI.
  15. WAC 182-505-0250, Washington apple health: MAGI-based adult medical
    Legislature. Date on it: Two versions shown: effective until January 1, 2027 (filed July 29, 2014), and effective January 1, 2027 (WSR 26-19-026, filed September 8, 2026). Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 on the Legislature's site, both versions shown there. Both say: age 19 or older and under 65. Not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare Part A or B. Not eligible for certain mandatory coverage. Meets the citizenship and immigration rule (WAC 182-503-0535) and the general eligibility rule (WAC 182-503-0505). Net countable income at or below 133 percent of the federal poverty level. The section does not use the figure 138 percent that HCA's fact sheet (WA05) uses, and it reaches residency only through the general eligibility rule. The version effective January 1, 2027 adds a 6-month certification period, 12 months for American Indians and Alaska Natives. The registry title was corrected to the rule's own caption on 2026-11-13. The caption has a dash where the title has a colon. The read confirms claim WA-WRD-02. It does not confirm the 138 percent figure in claim WA-CHK-01 as written.
  16. WAC 182-504-0035, Washington apple health: Renewals
    Legislature. Date on it: Two versions shown: effective September 6, 2025 until January 1, 2027, and effective January 1, 2027 (WSR 26-19-026, filed September 8, 2026). Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 on the Legislature's site, both versions shown there. Subsection (2)(b), for MAGI programs, until January 1, 2027: "you have 90 days from the termination date to give us a completed renewal. If we decide you are still eligible to get apple health coverage, we will restore your apple health without a gap in coverage." The same subsection effective January 1, 2027: "you have 90 days from the termination date to give us a completed renewal without requiring a new application." The sentence on restoring coverage without a gap is not in the 2027 version for MAGI programs. Both versions: the agency reconsiders without a new application when it gets the needed information within 30 days of the date on the termination notice, or when a hearing is requested within 90 days of the date on the renewal denial letter and a judge finds the decision wrong. The 2027 version adds renewals every six months for Apple Health for Adults, every 12 months for American Indians and Alaska Natives. The registry title was corrected to the rule's own caption on 2026-11-13. Claim WA-DEN-01 and the page text it feeds were corrected on 2026-11-13 to match both versions.
  17. WAC 182-504-0130, Washington apple health: Continued coverage pending an appeal
    Legislature. Date on it: Effective December 1, 2016 (WSR 16-22-060). Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 on the Legislature's site. To qualify for continued coverage, a person must request a hearing no later than the tenth day after the agency sent the notice, or the last day of the month before the action takes effect. A deadline that falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or designated holiday extends to 5:00 p.m. the next business day. Subsection (5), which no guide page states: a person cannot receive continued coverage if the action was due solely to a change in statute, federal regulation, or administrative rule, unless there is a question about whether the person is in the class of people affected by the change. The registry title was corrected to the rule's own caption on 2026-11-13. The read confirms claim WA-DEN-04.
  18. WAC 182-526-0200, Enrollee appeals of a managed care organization action
    Legislature. Date on it: Effective December 23, 2017 (WSR 17-23-201). Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 on the Legislature's site. An enrollee must exhaust the managed care organization's grievance and appeal process before requesting a hearing, and must request the hearing within 120 calendar days of the date of receipt of the organization's notice of resolution. To keep services going during the hearing, the request must be made within ten days of the date of that notice. Applies when the decision being challenged was made by a managed care organization rather than by HCA. The registry title was corrected to the rule's own caption on 2026-11-13. The read confirms claim WA-DEN-03; the rule counts the 120 days from receipt of the notice.
  19. WAC 182-503-0080, Washington apple health: Application denials and withdrawals
    Legislature. Date on it: Effective November 3, 2019. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 on the Legislature's site. The 90 days appears in subsection (4)(c): the agency reconsiders a denial without a new application when the person requests a hearing within ninety days of the date on the denial letter and an administrative law judge or HCA review judge decides the denial was wrong. Subsection (5) says a person who disagrees can ask for a hearing. The registry title was corrected to the rule's own caption on 2026-11-13. The read confirms claim WA-DEN-05; the 90 days run from the date on the denial letter.
  20. Medicaid in Washington State PDF
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: January 2026. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: FLAG, naming rule exception: the document's own published title contains the longer form of the state name. It is quoted exactly here because it is a source title. Guide wording must not copy it. Read in full on 2026-11-13: the text of all 46 slides. Charts and maps that are images were not read. The address is verified: it answered and served the deck on 2026-11-13. The first slide is headed "Impact of federal budget on Medicaid in Washington State, January 2026".   21. Medically Frail Exclusion from Community Engagement Requirements (memo to health care providers) PDF
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: August 21, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13 (4 pages). The address is verified: it answered and served the memo on 2026-11-13. The memo says: the Washington Healthplanfinder application will include a medical screener where a person can attest, under penalty of perjury, that they are medically frail. A code set is used in the background to verify the attestation. Self-declaration is allowed for eligibility decisions through 2027. Beginning in 2028, people may need to provide documentation when data sources cannot confirm their medical frailty. The memo names a diagnosis code list and method from the Harvard School of Public Health and lists ten Z codes. It does not state a 12-month look-back. The memo was written after the federal rule of June 1, 2026 and before the court order of Oct. 29, 2026 (S28). The medically frail code list is a separate spreadsheet on HCA's site, linked from WA22. It is not in this registry and was not read. No claim cites this source. Nothing from it is on a page.
  22. Apple Health (Medicaid) work requirements
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: No date shown. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13. The fetch returned the whole page this time. The page lists six ways to meet the rule: at least $580 in monthly household income. Working at least 80 hours a month. School at least half-time. A work program at least 80 hours a month. Community service at least 80 hours a month. Or a mix totaling at least 80 hours a month. It says the $580 is monthly household MAGI income. It separates "exemptions" (under 19, enrolled in Medicare Part A or B, enrolled in another Apple Health program, and four short-term hardships) from nine "current-month exclusions". It says a dependent child is under 14 through the month they turn 14, that the SNAP and TANF reasons depend on meeting those programs' work requirements, and that self-attestation of medical frailty is accepted through 2027. It words the medical frailty reason as "medically frail and are unable to work". It names one unemployment test for the hardship (1.5 times the national rate) and does not name 8 percent. At renewal it says a person must meet a requirement option in "any one month during your certification period". The screener survey it links to is a separate form and was not opened. The sample client notice and the fact sheet it links to were opened on the same day. No claim cites this source yet. Several of these points differ from what the guide now says. See docs/corrections.md, Washington items 2, 3, 4, 8, and 9.
  23. Apple Health Expansion
    Health Care Authority. Date on it: No date shown on the page. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13. The page says: "Enrollment is limited and the enrollment cap has been met." It links to an HCA announcement for updates. It says that from January 1, 2027 the program moves from managed care to coverage without managed care. It lists other coverage to check: Apple Health for Kids, Apple Health for pregnant individuals, After-Pregnancy Coverage, Alien Emergency Medical, Medical Care Services, and Family Planning Only. The page itself does not say the program is state-funded and does not show a December 2025 date. Its privacy section lists Apple Health Expansion among "Apple Health programs that get federal funding". Those two points are on the linked announcement ("Apple Health Expansion enrollment cap", dated Feb. 20, 2026, with an update headed December 2025, which says enrollment is closed and that the budget proposal continues state funds for the program). The announcement was read on 2026-11-13 and is not in this registry. The read confirms that enrollment is closed; it does not confirm claim WA-IMM-04 as written.
  24. Medicaid Community Engagement Requirement for Certain Individuals, Interim Final Rule with Comment Period (CMS-2454-IFC), fact sheet
    Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. Date on it: June 1, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-13. Read by us: Yes.
    Why we list it: Read in full on 2026-11-13. The registry address redirects to the same page at https. The fact sheet lists the short-term hardship kinds a state may offer: inpatient or certain other medical services. Travel outside the community for certain care not available at home, for the person or a dependent. A county with an unemployment rate at or above 8 percent or 1.5 times the national average. And a county where certain national emergencies or disasters exist. The rule itself is in the registry as shared source S27 and stays the primary federal source. The read confirms claim WA-RULE-09.
  25. Complaint, Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz, No. 1:26-cv-12962 (D. Mass.) PDF
    Commonwealth of Massachusetts (hosted filing). Date on it: Filed June 29, 2026. Checked: 2026-11-12. Read by us: No (read in part or not at all; see the note).
    Why we list it: Not read at this address. On 2026-11-13 the address answered 403 to two automated requests, and no further attempt was made. On the same day the text of the filed complaint (Document 1, filed 06/29/26, 74 pages, case 1:26-cv-12962) was read in full by the AI assistant from the copy the Washington Attorney General links at item 62 of its litigation tracker (WA14). One slide reproduced as an image in paragraph 79 came through as poor text. The exhibits are not part of that file and were not read. Why this guide exists

The facts about the new Apple Health rules are spread across many official pages and papers. This guide puts what applicants need in one place, in plain words. Each page lists the official sources behind it.

Independent. Not run by Washington's government, the Health Care Authority (HCA), or DSHS. Only Washington decides who meets the rules.

Private. No accounts, ads, cookies, or tracking. How your privacy is protected.
