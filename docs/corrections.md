# Where official sources disagree

This log lists places where official materials disagree with each other, with the federal rule, or with a court order. For each one it records what the site does. Source IDs (S01, U01, and so on) are defined in [sources.md](sources.md).

- [Oregon](#oregon): as found on **Nov. 2, 2026**. Item 1 was re-checked on Nov. 10, 2026.
- [Utah](#utah): as found on **Nov. 10, 2026**.

## Oregon

"Reported to OHA" stays "Not yet" until the maintainer decides to send it. OHA's documents list `one.communications@odhsoha.oregon.gov` for format and language requests; that is a reasonable place to start.

How the site chooses: follow the most authoritative current source (federal statute, then federal rule, then court orders on that rule, then state rules, then agency guidance), prefer the newest official statement, and say so in public. See [methodology.md](methodology.md).

### 1. The health condition test

- **S01 (OHA provider FAQ, undated):** says the federal government requires that a condition "qualifies only if it significantly limits the person's ability to work."
- **S03 (OHA exemptions explainer, undated):** describes qualifying conditions in terms of preventing part-time work.
- **S28 (court order, Oct. 29, 2026):** declares unlawful, vacates, and sets aside the part of the federal rule that defines "medically frail" to include a person "whose physical, mental, or other behavioral health condition significantly impairs the individual's ability to comply with the community engagement requirement," and bars applying it to the plaintiff states. **S35** confirms Oregon joined the suit.
- **S29 (court memorandum):** upheld the federal 12-month limit on claims data and the one-self-attestation-per-enrollment-period limit.
- **What the site does:** uses "a serious or disabling health condition," the wording on OHA's sample notice (S08), and adds an "In the news" note on the reasons and health pages. Checkpoint 2026-12-10 in [review.json](../data/review.json).
- **Inference:** the FAQ sentence tracks the clause the court vacated. As of Nov. 2, 2026, OHA's federal changes page (S07, last updated Sept. 18) had not said what changes.
- **Re-checked Nov. 10, 2026:** S01, S03, and S07 were unchanged, and S07 still showed "Last updated Sept. 18, 2026." The court entered judgment and dismissed the case on Oct. 29 (docket entry 149, S40). No notice of appeal appeared in the docket entries through Nov. 6 that CourtListener held. Next checkpoint: 2026-12-10.
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 2. What counts as school

- **S02 (fact sheet, June 1, 2026):** "Enrolled in higher education at least half-time."
- **S27 (federal rule, June 3, 2026):** "educational program" includes higher education and career and technical education, and the rule adds high school and state-approved high school equivalency (GED) programs.
- **S01 and S05:** say "school at least half-time" without limiting it to higher education.
- **What the site does:** "College, career school, high school, and GED programs can count." Source: S27.
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 3. Release from jail or prison

- **S02:** released "in the last 3 months."
- **S03:** within the last 4 months for new applicants, or 9 months for renewals.
- **Inference:** these may be the same 3-month rule counted from different dates, because OHP looks back 1 month at application and up to 6 months at renewal (S04, S07). Not confirmed by OHA.
- **What the site does:** uses S03's number for applicants (4 months).
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 4. The income test: "over" or "at least" $580

- **S01:** "More than $580 per month of household income." **S02:** "Income over $580 a month."
- **S27:** monthly income "not less than" the federal minimum wage times 80 hours, which is $7.25 x 80 = $580, counted as household income under MAGI rules.
- **What the site does:** "$580 or more." The difference matters only at exactly $580.
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 5. Former foster youth age

- **S02:** "under the age of 26." **S03:** "now 19 to 26."
- **What the site does:** "under 26."
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 6. The online application address

- **S09 (how-to-apply page):** "Go to ONE.Oregon.gov and click Apply Now."
- **S03, S05, S11:** benefits.oregon.gov.
- **What the site does:** links benefits.oregon.gov and notes that some state pages say ONE.Oregon.gov (inference: both are the same ONE system; not confirmed by a redirect test).
- **Reported to OHA:** Not yet.

### 7. Hearing deadline

- **S14 (OHA appeals and hearings page):** 60 calendar days from the date on OHA's Notice of Denial, written mainly about health service decisions.
- **S04 (journey maps):** for coverage decisions, "your notice lists how to request a hearing and the deadline."
- **What the site does:** tells people to follow the instructions and deadline on their own notice. Not a conflict, but an easy place to give the wrong number.

### 8. Undated documents

- **S01, S03, S04, S05, and S08** show no date. Readers cannot tell which is newest.
- **What the site does:** records the date each source was checked, and lists the date shown on it, in [sources.md](sources.md) and on every page.

### Not a conflict, but worth knowing

- The federal rule (S27) gives people at least 30 calendar days to respond after a notice of noncompliance. OHA's journey maps (S04) give 35 days to reply to a Request for Information. The site uses Oregon's 35 days, which is the letter applicants will actually get.
- OHP Bridge (S13): its page says nothing about whether the work or activity rules apply to Bridge members. The site does not say either way.

## Utah

Found on **Nov. 10, 2026**. Utah's documents were read through a tool that answers questions about a page instead of returning its full text (see [research-log.md](research-log.md)), so each item below should be confirmed by a person reading the documents before it is sent to the state.

"Reported to Utah" stays "Not yet" until the maintainer decides to send it. Utah's H.R. 1 page (U02) lists `dih_obbba@utah.gov` for policy questions; that is a reasonable place to start.

### 1. How many months a current member must meet the rules

- **U01 and U04:** "Current members - We look at your activities from your last renewal or approval until now." U04 also says the 80 hours apply "during each month of your lookback period."
- **U02:** "you must fulfill the community engagement requirement in at least one month since your last approval or renewal."
- **S27 (federal rule):** the part that sets the months for enrolled people (section II.H) is beyond the first 100,000 characters that were read. Unverified.
- **What the site does:** states only the rule for applicants, which every Utah page gives the same way (the month before you apply). It does not say how many months a current member must meet.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 2. Whose income counts toward $580

- **U01, U02, U04, U05, U07, U10:** "Earning at least $580 a month," with no word on whose earnings.
- **S27, section II.C.8:** "monthly income" is "the same as the individual's household income used for financial eligibility."
- **What the site does:** says "You earn at least $580 a month," Utah's wording, then says that Utah's pages do not say whose income counts, that the federal rule counts the household's, and to ask the state.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 3. Foster care

- **U01, U04, U05, U07:** "In foster care (or were in the past)."
- **U02:** "In foster care (or enrolled in the Former Foster Care program)." U03 says the same in substance.
- **What the site does:** "You are in foster care, or you were," with a note that some Utah pages name the Former Foster Care program, and to ask the state.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 4. Jail, prison, and treatment: exclusion or exception, and for how long

- **U01 and U02:** list people who are incarcerated now, and people "participating in a drug or alcohol treatment and rehabilitation program," among the exclusions. U01 separately counts incarceration "at any time during the 3 months before your last approval or renewal."
- **U05 (the table) and U04:** list people incarcerated or released from jail within the last 90 days under mandatory exceptions, and do not list treatment as its own line.
- **U07 (the member letter):** lists people who are incarcerated or were released from jail within the last 90 days.
- **What the site does:** "You are in jail or prison now, or you got out in the last 90 days," and lists treatment as its own reason, as U01 and U03 do.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 5. The general phone number

- **U01, U03, U14, U24, U25:** 1-866-608-9422 for other questions (the Medicaid Health Program Representative line).
- **U02 and U10 (the flyer):** 866-659-6176 for the same line.
- **What the site does:** lists 1-866-608-9422 and says some Utah pages list 866-659-6176 instead. Neither number was called.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 6. The age of a child in a caregiver's care

- **U11 (partner FAQ, January 2026):** "age 13 and younger" in one place, "under age 13" in another, and "under 14" in a third.
- **U01, U04, U05, U07:** "a child age 13 or younger."
- **What the site does:** "a child 13 or younger," the wording on Utah's current pages.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 7. Income numbers on the expansion page

- **U25:** the text gives $17,608 a year for one person and $36,156 for a family of 4 as 138% of the federal poverty level. The table on the same page gives $21,228 and $43,896.
- **What the site does:** copies neither. It links to the page's table, under the rule that numbers that change often are linked, not copied.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 8. Fax numbers for applications

- **U14 and the last page of U15 (form 61MED):** 801-526-9505 and toll-free 1-888-522-9505.
- **U16, U28, U03, and the cover sheet of U15:** 801-526-9500 and toll-free 1-877-313-4717.
- **What the site does:** on the apply page, tells people to use the number printed on their form or call DWS. On the letters page, gives 1-877-313-4717 and 801-526-9500 for sending papers, which is what Utah's letters and support page give for that purpose.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 9. Where the paper application asks about the rules

- **U03:** says members can report on application or renewal forms, and names Section H of the 61MED.
- **U15 (form 61MED, 04/01/2026):** Section H is "Health insurance information." No section asks about work, school, volunteering, or exclusions.
- **Inference:** a newer form may be coming.
- **What the site does:** tells people the paper form read on Nov. 10, 2026 does not ask about the rules. Checkpoint 2027-01-15.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 10. Whether Targeted Adult Medicaid ends

- **U08 (glossary, August 2026):** says TAM ends June 30, 2027, and its members move to Adult Expansion Medicaid.
- **U25:** says that effective July 1, 2027, TAM members may move to Adult Expansion. **U26:** changes take effect July 1, 2027; it does not say the program ends.
- **What the site does:** says what the glossary says and attributes it to the glossary. Checkpoint 2027-06-15.
- **Reported to Utah:** Not yet.

### 11. Dates on Utah's documents

- **U12:** the file name says January 2026; the footer says August 2025.
- **U27 (immigrant letter):** footer says March 2026. **U29 (the letter key):** says it was mailed in April 2026.
- **U01, U02, U03, U04, U14, U16, and others:** no date shown on the page.
- **What the site does:** records the date printed on each source and the date it was checked, in [sources.md](sources.md) and on every page.

### Not a conflict, but worth knowing

- **School.** U01 and U02 list going to school at least half-time among the activities that add up to 80 hours. The glossary (U08) and the flyer (U10) treat half-time enrollment as a way to meet the rules by itself, as the federal rule does. The site lists it as its own way.
- **Two hearing offices.** Utah Medicaid's hearings page (U17) is for disputes about services. It sends disputes about who qualifies to DWS. The site sends applicants to DWS.
- **The court ruling.** Utah was not a plaintiff in *Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Oz* (S40). The order (S28) sets aside one clause of the federal rule and bars the federal government from applying it to the plaintiff states. The site says what the court did and that Utah was not in the case, and does not say what the ruling means in Utah. Utah's pages did not mention it.
- **Utah's own waiver request.** U11 says Utah asked the federal government on July 3, 2025 for its own community engagement waiver. A federal letter of Sept. 25, 2026 (U30) changes Utah's demonstration to follow the federal requirement and does not approve a Utah-designed one. The site describes only the federal requirement as Utah's pages describe it.
- **Utah rule text.** One web search on Nov. 10, 2026 found no Utah Administrative Code filing that implements the requirement; a March 26, 2026 rules summary from Utah Medicaid listed none. Unverified.
