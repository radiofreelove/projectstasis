Have a health condition?

A disability or a health condition may mean you do not have to meet the new Utah rules.

The short version

  • Utah's words: medically frail or special needs.
  • 3 ways: Utah has 3 ways to confirm it. It tries them in order.
  • Records first: Utah Medicaid checks its own health records for you.
  • Treatment counts: Being in a drug or alcohol treatment program is its own reason.

What Utah lists

  • Blindness or a disability
  • A substance use disorder or a mental health condition
  • A condition that makes it hard to do daily tasks

The 3 ways Utah confirms it

  1. A records check. Utah Medicaid looks for health codes in the care it has paid for. You do nothing. If the check does not find your condition, that is not a denial.
  2. Your own answers. You fill out a short form about your condition. Utah calls it the Medically Frail Exclusion Screener. You sign that your answers are true.
  3. A provider form. A health care provider fills out a form for you. Utah asks for this only if the first 2 ways do not work.

Utah posts the list of health codes it checks PDF. We link to it but have not read it.

Hospital stays and medical travel

A stay in a hospital or care facility, or travel for medical care, may count as a short-term hardship. You must ask for these. See every reason
