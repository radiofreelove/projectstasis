The new rules, in plain words

This page explains who the rules are for and the 3 ways to meet them.

The short version

  • Starts: Jan. 1, 2027.
  • Who: Many adults ages 19 to 64 who apply for OHP.
  • Not for: People who get OHP because of a disability or pregnancy, or who have Medicare.
  • Healthier Oregon too: Adults in Healthier Oregon also have to meet the rules, unless a reason fits them.
  • When OHP looks: The month before you apply.
  • Good news: Many people already meet the rules, or don't have to.

Meet the rules any one way

1. Income

Your household brings in $580 or more a month.

That is about $135 a week.

It counts the income of everyone in your household, added together. Two adults do not each need $580.

Where $580 comes from: the federal minimum wage ($7.25 an hour) times 80 hours.

2. School

You go to school at least half-time.

Your school decides what counts as half-time. College, career school, high school, and GED programs can count.

3. 80 hours a month

You spend 80 hours a month on work, volunteering, school, or job training. You can add them together.

About 20 hours a week gets you there.

What counts as work

  • Paid work, including working for yourself or gig work.
  • Work paid in goods or housing, like managing a building for lower rent.
  • Unpaid work, like an internship.

What counts as volunteering

  • It has to be with a nonprofit or public program.
  • The program has to keep track of your hours.
  • Campaign work for a candidate does not count.
  • Helping one person, like a friend or neighbor, does not count.

Looking for work, school, or volunteer options? See OHP's job and volunteer resources.

Good to know

  • Each person counts: OHP checks each adult on their own. One person meeting the rules does not cover everyone in the home.
  • No monthly reports: You do not send proof every month. OHP reviews your case when you apply, and again before you renew.
  • Records first: OHP checks its own records before it asks you for anything.
